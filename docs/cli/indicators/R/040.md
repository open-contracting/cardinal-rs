# High share of buyer's contracts (R040)

A supplier's share of a buyer's awarded value is a high outlier.

:::{seealso}
{doc}`013`
:::

## Methodology

For each pair of a buyer and a supplier, the **share** is calculated as $valueAwardedToSupplierByBuyer \over valueAwardedByBuyer$ across all contracting processes, counting active awards that have an amount.

A supplier is flagged if its share is greater than or equal to the **upper fence** of $Q_3 + 1.5(IQR)$, where $Q_3$ is the third quartile and $IQR$ is the interquartile range for the set of shares. If a **threshold** is configured, it is used instead of the upper fence. The buyer is also flagged.

A buyer with fewer active awards than the **minimum** of 5 is excluded, because a buyer with a single contract would give its only supplier a share of 100%, which says nothing about favoritism.

An award to a consortium credits each of its suppliers with the award's full value, because OCDS does not record how the value is divided between them.

A supplier can hold a high share of more than one buyer's awards, and a buyer can concentrate its awards on more than one supplier. In either case, the highest share is reported.

Amounts in different currencies are never summed: a buyer's shares are calculated within each currency separately, and the resulting shares – being dimensionless – are compared together. If a `currency` is configured, awards in other currencies are skipped instead.

:::{note}
Because amounts are summed concurrently, a share can differ between runs in its last bits (on the order of 1e-16). Which contracting processes and organizations are flagged does not change.
:::

:::{admonition} Example
:class: seealso

25% ($Q_1$) of supplier-buyer pairs in Atlantis have a share of at most 1%. 75% ($Q_3$) have a share of at most 8%. This yields an upper fence of 18.5%.

The Ministry of Transport awarded \$1,000,000 in total, of which \$400,000 went to BuildFast Ltd. Its share is 40%. This is more than the upper fence of 18.5%. Therefore, BuildFast Ltd. and the Ministry of Transport are flagged.
:::

:::{admonition} Why is this a red flag?
:class: hint

A corrupt buyer can consistently award contracts to a preferred bidder.
:::

:::{admonition} Interpret with care
:class: caution

A concentrated market is not the same as a captured buyer. In a small jurisdiction, or for a specialized good with one national supplier, a high share is the expected outcome rather than a sign of favoritism. Read this indicator together with the number of suppliers that competed.
:::
